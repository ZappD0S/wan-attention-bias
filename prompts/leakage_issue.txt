I'm trying to manipulate the attention of Wan2.1 I2V to achieve a certain goal. My initial image contains two identical "characters", that can be whatever. In the initial image (the first frame) that I provide, these two characters are side by side and are identical.

I would like them to perform different actions independently. Simply describing this in the prompt is not enough, the model is not robust enogh to understand this correctly. So what I do is work on the cross-attention layer. I separately encode a sentence for each character describing the action that it should perform with the T5 encoder (the default for Wan2.1).

The cross-attention is first computed independently for every sentence. Enventually I mix toghether the cross-attention outputs by using certain masks.
These masks are obtained by repeating in time (i.e. for every frame) the bounding boxes for the two characters in the first frame (this is an approximation, but for characters not displacing it's fine). For the part that doesn't belongs to either characters (the "background"), I use another sentence, that simply describes the two characters but without any mention of the actions. What I mean by mixing is that the in the final output each the part that belongs to a certain character's mask is replaced with the attention output coming from the cross-attention calculated with the corresponding action description. There is no linear combination or "soft mixing". The issue that I get is that sometimes both characters perform the same action, or one of the two perform something that is a combination of both actions. Why is this happening in your opinion?
